# ADR 0018 · The field forms the CV as readable prose, not a CV-shaped logo

**Status:** accepted
**Date:** 2026-08-13
**Extends:** [ADR 0016](0016-continuous-shape-cycle.md) with a fifth shape

## Context

The CV is the document most visitors come to this site for, and until PRs #571
and #572 it was reachable only by typing `download cv` into the contact
terminal. Those two shipped the discoverable paths: a pill in the hero, a link
in every footer, and `/cv` as a readable page.

What remained was the repo owner's original idea, which was that the particle
field on the lander should form the CV as one of its shapes. The plan carried a
literal reading of that: rasterise the letters `CV` with an underline stroke,
in the same way the field already rasterises `mikkonumminen.dev`. A monogram.

The owner rejected that in favour of something better: form the **actual top of
the CV**, sharp enough to read, and let the part that cannot fit dissolve as
the particles fail to organise into words.

Two beliefs had to be tested before committing, because both could have killed
it:

- **Could 24,000 particles render body text at all?** Measured against the
  field's own raster: the two-line `MIKKO NUMMINEN` wordmark is ~94,000 ink
  pixels, and the entire CV block is ~26,000 sample points. Body text is
  **cheaper** than the name, because a 40px stroke is thick and a 2.5px one is
  not. The particle budget was never the constraint.
- **Would it be legible?** No, not at the field's default sprite size, and this
  is the finding that mattered. Rendered at 13px the glow bleeds ~5px past a
  body-text stroke: the counters of `a`, `e` and `o` fill in and words merge
  into a glowing ribbon. Rendered at 6px it reads cleanly. Compared at 13px,
  6px and 2.6px before any of this was built.

## Decision

Add `cv` as a **fifth shape**, showing the CV's name, title, contact line and
opening paragraph as readable prose, with the following paragraph scattering and
dimming into unresolved cloud.

### The fifth lane is appended, not inserted

`SHAPES` becomes `[name, galaxy, word, sparse, cv]` and a separate
`CYCLE_ORDER` carries the order shapes are actually shown in
(`name → galaxy → cv → word → sparse`).

Inserting `cv` at its show position would have renumbered `word` and `sparse`
across a `vec4` swizzle, eight per-shape tables, three modules and their tests.
Appending leaves every existing index untouched. The shader carries the first
four lanes in its existing `vec4` and the fifth in a scalar beside it.

ADR 0016's consequences section is the reason this got the care it did: *"A mask
that is slightly too permissive does not fail loudly."* A per-shape term that
reads only the first four lanes still compiles, still runs, and silently gives
the CV shape whatever the other four blended to.

Two mechanisms guard that, because a comment would not have:

- Every per-shape table is generated by one function (`glslShapeTable`) that
  emits the `vec4` and its `_CV` companion together and **throws** on a table
  that is not exactly as long as `SHAPES`.
- Every table is read through one GLSL helper, `shapeVal(w, w5, T, T_CV)`, and
  `fifthLane.test.ts` fails the build on a bare `dot(w, SHAPE_*)` anywhere in
  the shader, on a generated table that is never read through `shapeVal`, and on
  a missing `w5` term in either the geometry sum or the dust mask. All five of
  those failure modes were introduced deliberately and confirmed to fail the
  suite before this shipped.

### Sprite size becomes per-shape

A new `shapeSize` table exists entirely for this shape: `1` for the four
existing shapes, `0.45` for the CV. Bloom drops to `0.12` for the same reason
(bloom is what smears a thin stroke) and brightness rises to pay for the light a
smaller sprite loses.

### The CV block is fitted and sampled on its own terms

- Its canvas is 2600px wide against the name's 1400px, at the **same**
  world-per-pixel, because a canvas pixel is a fixed on-screen size and readable
  body text needs a ~40px canvas font that will not fit the narrower canvas.
- It has its own `uCvScale`, since it spans ~37 world units against the name's
  20 and needs a different reduction to fit.
- It samples at **1px** where every other shape samples at 2px. Not a quality
  knob: at 2px the block yields fewer sample points than it has particles, and
  the distributor resolves that surplus by stacking three particles on each
  point. The strokes stayed thinly covered and the block read dim at any
  brightness.
- It is mapped around the centre of its **ink** rather than of its canvas, which
  is what keeps it clear of the hero masthead.

### Held longer, and skipped when it cannot be read

`shapeHold` becomes a per-shape table so the CV holds 11 s against everyone
else's 5 s. Five seconds is ample for a wordmark and nowhere near enough to read
a paragraph, which is the entire point of this shape.

The shape is skipped, not degraded, when it cannot work:

- **Low-performance tier** (8,000 particles at a 17px sprite) never rasterises
  it. A half-resolved CV is worse than no CV.
- **Small viewports.** The field maps a fixed world span onto the window, so the
  body text shrinks with it; below 15 CSS px the formation is a smear rather
  than a document. Re-evaluated on every resize rather than captured once,
  because a window drag changes the answer mid-cycle.
- **Raster failure**, same null-means-skip contract the wordmark already uses.

Because two of the five shapes can now be unavailable at once, skipping is a
bounded scan rather than a single hop: one step from the galaxy with both text
shapes unavailable would land on the other unavailable shape and morph the field
into a zero-filled attribute, collapsing it onto the origin.

## Consequences

**The formation is decorative by definition.** The canvas is `aria-hidden`, so
text rendered as particles reaches no screen reader. That is acceptable only
because the same CV is real DOM at `/cv` and a real link in the hero and footer;
it would not be acceptable as the only copy.

**Per-particle cost rises.** Two new attributes (`aCvPos`, `aCvDim`) take the
field from 72 to 88 bytes per particle, about 2.1 MB at 24,000. The tick loop is
unchanged and still allocation-free: the shape costs uniform writes, not CPU
work per frame.

**The dim channel is now continuous.** It was a 0/1 glyph-or-dust flag; the CV
block's fade rides it as a real number. Nothing in the shader assumed the flag
was binary, and `distributeNameTargets` now takes an optional per-candidate dim
array, but a future reader should not assume it is a boolean.

**The block's copy is duplicated.** The prose in `cvTargets.ts` is a hand-copied
extract of `content/cv.md` and can drift from it. Reading the markdown was
rejected: this module is bundled to the browser, the field can only ever show a
few hundred characters, and shipping the whole document to discard 95% of it is
a worse trade than a short extract that is visibly an extract.

**Clicking the formation does not download the CV yet.** It strikes the shape
like every other, using its measured ink bounds. The transparent overlay
`<a download>` over the projected block is deliberately separate work.
